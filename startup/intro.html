Startup Foundations

This introduction teaches how real businesses are built — through problem-solving, execution, and disciplined learning.

1. What a Startup Actually Is

A startup is not a small business. It is a temporary organization designed to search for a repeatable and scalable business model.

The goal is learning before growth.

2. Choosing the Right Problem

Successful startups solve painful problems for specific users. Ideas matter less than insight into real user needs.

A boring problem with urgency beats an exciting problem without demand.

3. Validation Before Building

Validation means confirming demand before heavy investment. This can involve interviews, pre-orders, or small experiments.

Early feedback saves time and capital.

4. Execution and Iteration

Progress comes from building, measuring, and adjusting. Speed matters, but direction matters more.

Startups succeed by learning faster than competitors.

5. Risk and Sustainability

Most startups fail due to poor execution, not lack of ideas. Managing burn rate and expectations increases survival odds.

Longevity creates optionality.
