Investing Introduction: Building Wealth Fundamentals

Learn what investing really means, difference between saving/trading/investing, stocks/ETFs basics, compound interest, and beginner mistakes to avoid.

Progress: 1 of 4 lessons complete

What Investing Really Means

Investing is allocating money with the expectation of generating an income or profit. Unlike saving (preserving money) or trading (short-term speculation), investing focuses on long-term wealth building.

Core Principles:

  • Time in the market beats timing the market - Consistency matters more than perfect timing
  • Risk and return are related - Higher potential returns come with higher risk
  • Diversification reduces risk - Don't put all your eggs in one basket
  • Investing is a marathon, not a sprint - Long-term perspective is key

Saving vs Trading vs Investing

Saving (Preservation)

  • Goal: Preserve capital, maintain liquidity
  • Timeframe: Short-term (0-3 years)
  • Risk: Very low (FDIC insured accounts)
  • Returns: Low (0.5-2% typically)
  • Examples: Savings accounts, CDs, money market funds

Trading (Speculation)

  • Goal: Profit from short-term price movements
  • Timeframe: Very short-term (seconds to months)
  • Risk: Very high
  • Returns: Variable, often negative for beginners
  • Examples: Day trading, swing trading, options trading

Investing (Wealth Building)

  • Goal: Build wealth over the long term
  • Timeframe: Long-term (5+ years)
  • Risk: Moderate (managed through diversification)
  • Returns: Historically 7-10% annually in stock market
  • Examples: Retirement accounts, index funds, dividend stocks

Stocks, ETFs, Bonds Basics

Stocks (Equities)

  • What: Ownership shares in a company
  • Returns: Dividends + capital appreciation
  • Risk: Medium to High
  • Best for: Long-term growth
  • Beginner tip: Start with well-established companies

ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)

  • What: Baskets of stocks traded like individual stocks
  • Returns: Diversified market returns
  • Risk: Low to Medium (due to diversification)
  • Best for: Beginners, diversified exposure
  • Beginner tip: Start with S&P 500 index ETFs

Bonds (Fixed Income)

  • What: Loans to governments or corporations
  • Returns: Fixed interest payments
  • Risk: Low to Medium
  • Best for: Income, portfolio stability
  • Beginner tip: Start with government bonds

Compound Interest Explained

Compound interest is often called the "eighth wonder of the world" because it allows your money to grow exponentially over time.

How It Works:

  • You earn interest on your initial investment (principal)
  • You also earn interest on the interest you've already earned
  • This creates exponential growth over long periods

The Rule of 72

A quick way to estimate how long it takes for an investment to double:

  • Divide 72 by your annual return rate
  • Example: At 7% return, money doubles every ~10 years (72 ÷ 7 = 10.3)
  • Example: At 10% return, money doubles every ~7 years (72 ÷ 10 = 7.2)

The Power of Starting Early

  • Start at 25: Invest $300/month at 7% for 40 years = $719,000
  • Start at 35: Invest $300/month at 7% for 30 years = $340,000
  • Start at 45: Invest $300/month at 7% for 20 years = $148,000

Starting 10 years earlier more than doubles your result!

Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

1. Trying to Time the Market

Mistake: Waiting for the "perfect" time to invest
Solution: Invest regularly regardless of market conditions (dollar-cost averaging)

2. Chasing Hot Tips

Mistake: Investing based on rumors or social media hype
Solution: Do your own research, invest in what you understand

3. Lack of Diversification

Mistake: Putting all money in one stock or sector
Solution: Spread investments across different assets

4. Letting Emotions Drive Decisions

Mistake: Selling in panic during downturns
Solution: Have a plan and stick to it through market cycles

5. Not Considering Fees

Mistake: Ignoring management fees and transaction costs
Solution: Use low-cost index funds and discount brokers

6. Waiting Until You Have "Enough" Money

Mistake: Thinking you need thousands to start
Solution: Start with whatever you can afford, even $50/month
