Tech Investing Foundations

This introduction teaches how technology-driven businesses create value and how investors can participate responsibly.

1. What Investing Actually Is

Investing is the allocation of capital into productive assets with the expectation of future returns. In technology, returns often come from scalability and network effects.

Speculation focuses on price. Investing focuses on value creation.

2. Why Technology Dominates Modern Markets

Technology businesses benefit from low marginal costs and global reach. This allows successful products to scale rapidly once product-market fit is achieved.

Investors seek companies that compound value over time.

3. Risk, Time, and Patience

Higher potential returns come with uncertainty. Long-term investing rewards patience and discipline more than frequent trading.

Understanding volatility prevents emotional decisions.

4. Valuation Basics

Valuation compares price to expected future cash flows. Even great companies can be poor investments at extreme prices.

Margin of safety matters.

5. Building a Learning-Based Portfolio

Beginners should focus on diversified exposure, continuous learning, and avoiding overconfidence. Knowledge compounds just like capital.

The goal is sustainability, not shortcuts.
