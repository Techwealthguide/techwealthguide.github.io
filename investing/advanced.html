Advanced Tech Investing

This section focuses on decision quality, capital allocation, and avoiding common investor failure modes.

1. Thinking in Probabilities

Investing outcomes are probabilistic, not deterministic. Great decisions can have poor short-term outcomes.

Long-term success comes from consistently making decisions with positive expected value.

2. Capital Allocation Frameworks

The best investors allocate capital based on opportunity cost. Cash, equities, and alternatives compete for allocation.

Concentration increases returns and risk simultaneously.

3. Moats and Competitive Advantage

Sustainable returns require durable advantages: network effects, switching costs, scale, or brand.

Temporary advantages fade quickly in technology markets.

4. Valuation Under Uncertainty

Precision is impossible. Ranges matter more than point estimates. Stress-testing assumptions reveals hidden risk.

Conservative assumptions protect capital.

5. Behavioral Traps

Overconfidence, recency bias, and narrative addiction destroy returns. Systems and checklists reduce emotional mistakes.

Discipline compounds quietly.
